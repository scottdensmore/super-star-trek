### `sed -E`, `#` delimiter, and `/` inside the bracket on BSD/macOS

I can give you a confident answer without a Mac:

1. **`-E` is the safest possible choice.** It is BSD-*native* (macOS `sed` documents `-E` for extended regular expressions), GNU sed has accepted it since 4.2, and it is now standardised. The spelling that would have broken macOS is `-r`, which you did not use.
2. **The `#` delimiter removes the exact ambiguity you were worried about.** POSIX permits any character but backslash and newline as the `s` delimiter. The historically murky area is whether a bracket expression suppresses delimiter scanning when *the delimiter itself* appears inside brackets — implementations have differed. By using `#`, no `#` occurs anywhere in the regex or replacement, so `/` inside the bracket is just an ordinary literal with no special status under any implementation. Your instinct was right and the result is strictly safer than `/`-delimited.
3. **`([^A-Za-z0-9._/-]|$)` is portable ERE.** Grouping and alternation are standard; in ERE (unlike BRE) `$` is special *anywhere*, so `(x|$)` is well-defined on BSD; a trailing `-` in a bracket expression is literal per POSIX. GNU sed and busybox sed produce **byte-identical output** across a 10-line adversarial corpus (verified with `cmp`).
4. **The failure mode is loud, not silent.** If BSD sed diverged on any of this, the substitution simply would not blank the allowed import, and the *baseline repository* would fail the check on the very first macOS run. I confirmed this directly: with the substitution treated as a no-op, the real `CLAUDE.md` matches the grep and fails. There is no path to a silent false pass on macOS.
