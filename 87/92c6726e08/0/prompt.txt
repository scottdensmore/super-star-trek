## Answers to your design questions

**Is there an era convention to match? No — stop looking for one.** The 1978 lineage this code descends from (Leedom's HP-2000 BASIC → Almy's C) is text-only; the contemporaneous Apple II / Atari / TRS-80 ports coloured things idiosyncratically and did not agree with each other. There is no canonical SST colour language to match, and I'd rather tell you that than invent one. What *does* exist and is worth honouring is two conventions, one of which you already have: Star Trek's own alert language (green/yellow/red for condition — you nailed this) and the tactical-grid convention shared by roguelikes and 4X minimaps (the player is the most distinct thing on the map; threats are warm; terrain is muted; unknown is grey). Judged against the second one, your grid gets threats and unknown right and terrain wrong.

**Stars yellow / planets green — yes, you have it backwards, but not for the reason you guessed.** Your instinct was "danger should drive the palette." I'd put it more precisely: **salience should track scarcity × decision-relevance, and a hue must mean one thing across both panels.** In the quadrants I sampled, stars ran 7–9 per grid against 1 hostile. Yellow is the second-loudest colour available and you're spending it on the most common object, so the grid reads as a field of yellow with a red letter hidden in it. The clincher is the cross-panel collision: yellow is your `STAT_WARN` — shields down, life support on starbase support — so on the right-hand panel yellow means *pay attention*, and eight inches to the left it means *ordinary scenery*. That is the one genuine inconsistency in the scheme.

I'd make stars the quietest thing that isn't empty space (plain, or blue if you want them distinct), keep planets green (they're an objective — dilithium — so "green = something good here" agrees with the status panel's green), and reserve yellow for actual caution. That also frees yellow up coherently if you ever colour Time Left. The counter-argument is real and you should weigh it: a yellow star is *pictorially* right, there's charm in that, and this is a game rather than a dashboard. But the yellow-means-two-things problem is what tips it for me.

**Bold white for the ship — no, and this is the finding I'd fix first.** See should-fix 1: on a light-background terminal it's white on white.

**All hostiles the same red — keep it.** On a 10x10 grid with five hostile types, "everything red is trying to kill you" is the right level of detail. The letters already carry the identity, the game teaches them, and the message log names them in prose. Shading them would demand colour distinctions that collapse on 8-colour terminals and would compete with the red/not-red signal that actually drives decisions. One optional refinement if you ever want it: give the **Super-commander alone** something extra (`A_REVERSE`, say). "The Super-commander is in this quadrant" is the single most consequential piece of tactical news in the game, and it's the one case where the letter doing all the work undersells it. Polish, not a defect.

**Dim for masked cells — keep it, it reads as "hidden".** Dim/grey is the standard "no data" idiom and, crucially, it can't be misread as an object. It also degrades safely: `A_DIM` is unimplemented on a fair number of terminals, but since `-` appears nowhere else in the grid the cell still reads as unknown from the glyph alone. Good choice.

**Only three status lines — right restraint, but add exactly one.** A panel where everything is coloured is a panel where nothing is, so I'd resist Energy and Torpedoes: Energy is already mirrored by Condition (YELLOW fires at `energy &lt; 1000`, which is precisely the threshold you'd colour Energy at, so you'd be saying the same thing twice on adjacent lines), and a torpedo count of 0 is legible as a small integer. **Time Left is different.** I grepped for it: there is no low-time warning anywhere in the game — no prose, no event, nothing. Running out of time is a primary lose condition and the status panel is its only channel. Yellow under ~20% of `intime` and red under ~10% would add information rather than repeat it. That's the one field where colour would tell a player something the game currently never says.

**Accessibility — you pass the test that matters, and here's why it's worth saying out loud.** Colour is *redundant everywhere*: every coloured status value also spells its state in words (GREEN/YELLOW/RED, DOCKED, ACTIVE/DAMAGED, UP/DOWN/DAMAGED), and every coloured grid cell is already a distinct glyph (K/C/S/R/T/E/F/B/\*/P/#/-). So a red-green colour-blind player loses nothing — they read exactly what a monochrome player reads. That is the correct design and it's easy to get wrong, so it deserves a comment in the code saying it's deliberate. The real accessibility failures are all light-background contrast, below.

## Findings

**Should-fix 1 — the ship is invisible on a light-background terminal.** `tui.c`, `init_pair(CP_SHIP, COLOR_WHITE, bg)` with `bg = -1`. The branch's stated principle is that the panels "sit in the player's colour scheme", but hardcoding `COLOR_WHITE` as a *foreground* silently assumes the background is dark. On Solarized Light, `xterm -rv`-less defaults, or any white-background terminal, the player's own ship renders white on white. Yellow stars suffer the same way — yellow on white is notoriously low contrast, so the two most numerous coloured things on the grid both fail together. Fix for the ship: drop the pair entirely and use `A_BOLD` alone, which keeps the terminal's own foreground and simply brightens it — bright white on dark, bold black on light, always legible, always distinct from the plain dots, and one fewer colour pair. That's a strictly better implementation of the intent you already had.

**Should-fix 2 — the Thing (`?`) is unclassified, and the game explicitly tells the player to go look at it.** `cell_class()` has no case for `IHQUEST`, so it falls to `CELL_PLAIN`. But `setup.c:680` drops a `?` into the quadrant and prints:

**Nit 3 — the black hole's invisibility is correct but undocumented, and someone will "fix" it.** `IHBLANK` (`' '`) also falls to `CELL_PLAIN`, and `setup.c:692` drops up to three per quadrant. **Nit 4 — honour `NO_COLOR`.** `start_colour()` decides purely on `has_colors()`. Checking `getenv("NO_COLOR")` before `start_color()` is three lines and is becoming the de-facto standard for CLI tools; it's also the courtesy that low-vision users and monochrome-preferring players actually reach for.

**Nit 5 — a note on `use_default_colors()` failing.** The `bg = COLOR_BLACK` fallback is sensible, but on a light-background terminal that lacks default-colour support it paints black cells behind every coloured glyph, producing a patchwork against the uncoloured dots.
